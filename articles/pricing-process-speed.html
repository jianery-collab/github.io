Pricing Process

Is your pricing process slowing you down?

A pricing process should expedite decisions, not create roadblocks and layers of escalation.

Jan Y. Yang · pricinggoat.com

In today's fast-paced market environment, pricing is one of the most critical levers that businesses can pull to drive revenue and profitability. Yet pricing is often treated as an afterthought or, worse, it gets bogged down in layers of approvals, manual inputs, and inefficient workflows.

Implementing a structured pricing process is essential for companies to remain competitive and agile. But that process must be designed to expedite decisions, not to create additional roadblocks or layers of escalation.

The need for speed in pricing decisions

The rapid pace of market change, driven by global competition, technological innovation, and shifting consumer preferences, means that pricing decisions need to happen quickly. Companies that rely on slow, cumbersome approval chains risk missing opportunities to adjust prices in real time, whether to take advantage of market dynamics or to respond to competitive pressures.

A robust pricing process provides the framework for making quick, well-informed decisions. It should standardize inputs, leverage historical data, and integrate real-time market insights to generate pricing options without the need for excessive manual intervention. Automating these elements ensures that pricing teams can focus on strategic decisions, not on administrative tasks.

Automate where you can, focus on what matters

One of the most critical tenets of an effective pricing process is simple:

Everything that can be automated, should be automated.

This isn't just about efficiency. It's about freeing up people to focus on the strategic and complex aspects of pricing decisions that truly require human judgment.

Automating basic tasks like price updates, competitive benchmarking, and discount calculations allows the team to concentrate on analyzing customer behavior, market trends, and product positioning. This approach not only accelerates decision-making but also improves accuracy by reducing the risk of human error.

For example, a pricing tool can automatically update prices based on predefined business rules such as competitive pricing, demand fluctuations, or inventory levels. This keeps prices in sync with the market without the need for manual reviews. When intervention is needed, such as when launching a new product or entering a new market, the pricing team can devote its full attention to those high-impact decisions.

Avoiding the "escalation trap"

One common mistake businesses make when building a pricing process is adding too many layers of approval. While it's important to have governance and oversight, excessive escalation creates bottlenecks that slow down decision-making and can lead to missed opportunities.

A well-designed pricing process should have clear guidelines that enable decisions to be made at the lowest possible level. For routine pricing adjustments, such as those driven by market changes or promotions, approval can be handled by automated systems. For more complex or high-stakes decisions, the process should quickly escalate the decision to the appropriate level without unnecessary delays.

It's vital to strike the right balance between control and agility. While you don't want to lose oversight, over-escalating can reduce pricing flexibility and frustrate both employees and customers. A decentralized pricing structure, with built-in automation and oversight at key touchpoints, allows companies to maintain control without slowing down the process.

Driving consistency without sacrificing flexibility

A formal pricing process brings consistency across the organization, ensuring that decisions align with broader business goals like margin targets or market share objectives. However, this consistency should not come at the expense of flexibility. An effective pricing process should be agile enough to adapt to the specific needs of different products, markets, or customer segments.

For example, the pricing of a commodity product may be largely automated and data-driven, with little need for human intervention. A highly customized product or service, on the other hand, may require more nuanced pricing that accounts for individual customer negotiations or unique market conditions. The pricing process should accommodate both, allowing flexibility where needed while maintaining overall consistency.

Data-driven decision making

An efficient pricing process also leverages data to make informed decisions. Whether through historical sales data, competitive analysis, or willingness-to-pay models, having access to the right data ensures that pricing decisions are grounded in reality. By incorporating automated data analytics into the process, businesses can reduce the time spent gathering and analyzing information, speeding up decision-making.

Moreover, data-driven insights enable dynamic pricing strategies. In e-commerce, for example, algorithms can adjust prices based on real-time supply and demand, competitive activity, or customer behavior. This type of automation allows companies to maximize revenue opportunities without manual intervention.

Implementing a scalable process

A pricing process should be scalable and adaptable as the business grows and evolves. Startups and smaller businesses may begin with a relatively simple process that involves manual oversight for most decisions. However, as the business expands, so too must the pricing process.

Automation plays a key role in scaling. As the number of SKUs, customer segments, or geographic markets grows, automating routine tasks like price updates, discount approvals, and competitor analysis becomes essential. A scalable pricing process ensures that the business can manage increasing complexity without sacrificing speed or accuracy.

Streamlining, not stalling.

The goal of a pricing process should be to accelerate decisions, not slow them down. By automating routine tasks, eliminating unnecessary escalation, and leveraging data-driven insights, companies can ensure that their pricing process is both efficient and flexible. A well-designed pricing process strikes a balance between governance and agility, allowing for quick, informed pricing decisions that keep pace with market dynamics.

In the end, companies that invest in an efficient pricing process will not only make better pricing decisions but also gain a competitive edge by responding more rapidly to changes in the market. The key is to automate wherever possible, focus people on high-impact decisions, and avoid creating unnecessary bottlenecks that hinder agility.
